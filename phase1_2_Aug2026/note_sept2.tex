\noindent
This note answers the four open empirical questions: what flips the
myopia result, whether the flat parameters are an estimation artifact,
what the routing theory says at the calibrated primitives, and the status
of the two numerical properties the theory documents asked us to verify.
Code and full results: \texttt{github.com/hosseinpiri/recommendation-systems},
folder \texttt{phase1\_2\_Aug2026}.

\section{Boosting $\rho$, and the real gate}

Raising $\rho$ counterfactually does flip the result, monotonically: the
aware-minus-myopic gap runs from \rGapBase\% at the calibrated
$\rho=0.035$ (statistically zero) to \rGapOne\% at $\rho=1$, where the
primitive route used as a policy captures \rRoute\%, nearly the full
premium, so the route is a near-optimal policy wherever cultivation has
value. But one percent at $\rho=1$ is a cap, and the phase map explains
it: crossing intercept-spread multipliers $\{1,3,6\}$ with
$\kappa\in\{1.966,4,6\}$ at $\rho=0.45$, a sixfold intercept spread moves
the gap from \phKhat\% only to \phSpreadSix\%, while $\kappa=6$ at the
original spread moves it to \phKsix\%. The near-uniform consumption
probabilities are a symptom of small $\kappa$, not of close intercepts;
alignment discrimination is the single gate behind every null so far.

\section{The estimation is not forcing the data}

Regenerating click labels on the real design matrix from known truths
(intercept spreads up to six times ours, $\kappa$ up to 6, all recentred
to the 4\% click rate), the production estimation recovers $\kappa$ to
within \recKerr\ and the intercept profile with correlation at least
\recCorr\ in all nine configurations. If tastes here were heterogeneous
and alignment-sensitive, the pipeline would have said so; the flat
calibration is a property of news reading, not of the method.

\section{The routing theory predicts our null}

The $\rho=1$ theory is directly computable on the 14-category graph. The
skip inequality
$1/p_{j\to k}+1/p_{k\to i}-m_k/\bar r_{a^\star}\le 1/p_{j\to i}$ holds for
none of the 2{,}184 ordered triples at calibration (minimum slack 17): no
stepping stone pays, so the optimal route is direct harvesting, which is
myopia. The dense content Gram admits \gBridgesDense\ profitable triples,
but the turnpike threshold is $\bar H\approx\gHbarOne$ (one-hot) and
$\approx\gHbarDense$ (dense) against 30-period sessions, and patience
breakpoints start at retention $\delta\approx\gBreakMin$ in the $\rho=1$
idealization: sessions this short play myopically by the theory's own
account. (Frontier notes: one-hot features make the full dominance frontier
structurally 100\%; the meaningful $(\alpha,m)$-restricted frontier is
\{\gFrontierAM\}, and the artificial $\alpha=-10$ item joins it on
margin alone, confirming the documented non-tightness.)

\section{The two conjectured properties fail, in scope}

Over 2{,}000 random instances (exact node recursion, $H=60$),
\cxInScope\ satisfy Assumption 2 in both stated forms. Within scope, the
ordered-marginals inequality of Remark 7 fails in \cxUpper\ instances in
its upper half and \cxTwoSided\ in its full two-sided form; single
crossing fails at a comparable rate, with up to \cxCrossMax\ sign changes
in one instance. The violations occur deep in the turnpike regime, sit
nine orders above float noise, and an independent audit reproduced every
count and verified the saved examples with separate code. Three complete
counterexample instances, each certified in scope, are in the shared
results for Xinyuan. The constructive reading: the exact ladder and the
interval conjecture need a primitive condition defining a subclass, and
that search is now example-guided rather than open-ended.


\section{Why the parameters look uniform: they are not}

Two follow-up estimations answer the uniformity question directly. First,
the flat intercepts are largely aggregation: refitting with subcategory
intercepts (\subN\ subcategories with sufficient data), the spread is
\subSpread\ log-odds (\subPspread\ between the 5th and 95th percentiles),
five times the category-level 0.85; averaging heterogeneous article types
into 14 categories is what flattened the world. Second, the transition is
not one $\rho$: per-category profiling gives $\rho_c\approx 0.05$ for
news, sports, and food, 0.02 for finance and entertainment, and
$\rho_c\approx 0$ for travel and health (likelihood ratio \rhoTravelLR\
against the common value for travel alone). The pattern reads as
recency-driven versus need-driven content, and it strengthens the
persistent-interest interpretation of $\rho$: categories with bursty
topics show updating, categories serving stable needs show none. Both
findings argue for a finer action space in the next dataset round.

\section{Resistance and the index, revised}

\textbf{Resistance: exists, and small, which is what the theory needs.}
On the full impression scale, jointly estimated with $\rho$, backfire is
decisively present (the simplex-native proportional-redistribution form
is data-preferred, likelihood ratio 1{,}489) with a per-impression
magnitude of 0.002 to 0.01, attention-diluted, far too small to threaten
the routing structure. Its policy value is material only when paired with
$\kappa\ge5$. Proposal: adopt it as a perturbation term in the
proportional form, keep the no-resistance case as the solved baseline,
and cite the measured magnitude as the smallness justification.

\textbf{Index policy: the route is the missing potential.} The route
value $W$ supplies a potential with a certified error bound where the
earlier geometric guess reached 97\% of oracle and the myopic-payoff
potential failed. The benchmark to beat remains estimate-then-plug-in
planning, near-oracle whenever a few persistent intercepts are the only
unknowns, so the index's case should be built where plug-in struggles:
state uncertainty or short-lived items, on the route-based $\Phi$.
