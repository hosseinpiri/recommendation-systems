\noindent
This note answers the meeting's two open empirical questions, what flips
the myopia result (Meichun) and whether the flat parameters are an
estimation artifact (Xinyuan), and adds two initiatives of my own:
computing the routing theory directly at the calibrated primitives, and a
numerical audit of two properties that Xinyuan's August 19 notes describe
as numerically observed but unproven, taken up under his invitation to
stress-test the drafts.
Code and full results: \texttt{github.com/hosseinpiri/recommendation-systems},
folder \texttt{phase1\_2\_Aug2026}.

\section{Boosting $\rho$, and the real gate}

Raising $\rho$ counterfactually, Meichun's suggestion, does flip the
result, monotonically: the
aware-minus-myopic gap runs from \rGapBase\% at the calibrated
$\rho=0.035$ (statistically zero) to \rGapOne\% at $\rho=1$, where the
primitive route used as a policy captures \rRoute\%, nearly the full
premium, so the route is a near-optimal policy wherever cultivation has
value. But one percent at $\rho=1$ is a cap, and the phase map explains
it: crossing intercept-spread multipliers $\{1,3,6\}$ with
$\kappa\in\{1.966,4,6\}$ at $\rho=0.45$, operationalizing Xinyuan's
$\alpha$ and $\kappa$ perturbations, a sixfold intercept spread moves
the gap from \phKhat\% only to \phSpreadSix\%, while $\kappa=6$ at the
original spread moves it to \phKsix\%. The near-uniform consumption
probabilities (average 4\%, nearly common across items) are a symptom of
small $\kappa$, not of close intercepts; alignment discrimination is the
single gate behind every null so far. Two loose ends from the same
discussion close the same way: removing $\alpha$ entirely, refitting with
a single common intercept, gives $\kappa=1.34$ and simulated gaps of
0.03\% at $\hat\rho$ and 1.12\% at $\rho=0.45$, so nothing was driven
by the intercepts; and the general-$\sigma$ question is answered by the
spline diagnostics, where freeing the functional form improves holdout
log loss only in the fourth decimal, so the logistic is not the
constraint either.

The sweep also extends past full adoption. For $\rho\in(1,2)$ the
transition is well defined, $T_i(z)-x_i=(1-\rho)(z-x_i)$ places the state
on the far side of the consumed item while contracting at rate $\rho-1$
(reflection at $\rho=2$, divergence beyond), and the tube machinery
carries over with radius $|1-\rho|D$. In the showcase this overshoot
regime is a slingshot: the exact-DP premium rises from \osToyOne\% at
$\rho=1$ to \osToyMax\% at $\rho=1.95$ and the optimal route shortens
from two bridges to one. The data want none of it: in the category
simplex the projected transition saturates at the clicked vertex for
every $\rho\ge1$, making overshoot empirically indistinguishable from
full adoption, and the entire region is rejected against
$\hat\rho=0.035$ by \osDrop\ log-likelihood points. Overshooting belongs
in the theory's parameter map, not in the news calibration.

\section{The estimation is not forcing the data}

Regenerating click labels on the real design matrix from known truths
(intercept spreads up to six times ours, $\kappa$ up to 6, all recentred
to the 4\% click rate), the production estimation recovers $\kappa$ to
within \recKerr\ and the intercept profile with correlation at least
\recCorr\ in all nine configurations. If tastes here were heterogeneous
and alignment-sensitive, the pipeline would have said so; the flat
calibration is a property of news reading, not of the method.

\section{The routing theory predicts our null}

The $\rho=1$ theory is directly computable on the 14-category graph. The
skip inequality
$1/p_{j\to k}+1/p_{k\to i}-m_k/\bar r_{a^\star}\le 1/p_{j\to i}$ holds for
none of the 2{,}184 ordered triples at calibration (minimum slack 17): no
stepping stone pays, so the optimal route is direct harvesting, which is
myopia. The dense content Gram admits \gBridgesDense\ profitable triples,
but the threshold horizon above which the route logic applies at all is
$\bar H\approx\gHbarOne$ (one-hot) and $\approx\gHbarDense$ (dense)
against 30-period sessions, and patience
breakpoints start at retention $\delta\approx\gBreakMin$ in the $\rho=1$
idealization: sessions this short play myopically by the theory's own
account. Xinyuan's artificial-item test asked whether breaking the uniformity
of consumption probabilities produces a nontrivial policy: inserting an
$\alpha=-10$, margin-5 category makes the probabilities sharply
non-uniform, yet the skip inequality still holds for no triple and the
optimal route stays myopic; non-uniformity alone is not enough at our
$\kappa$, exactly as the phase map predicts. (Frontier notes: one-hot
features make the full dominance frontier structurally 100\%; the
meaningful $(\alpha,m)$-restricted frontier is \{\gFrontierAM\}, and
the artificial item joins it on margin alone, confirming the documented
non-tightness.)

\section{The two conjectured properties fail, in scope}

Xinyuan's two August 19 notes, \emph{Optimal Path} (routing structure
for the discounted and finite-time settings) and \emph{Efficient
Frontier} (dominance and route characterization), describe two properties
as numerically observed but unproven: the ordered-marginals inequality of
Remark 7 in \emph{Optimal Path} (``one lemma would settle three
things'') and the single-crossing property behind Conjecture 1 in
\emph{Efficient Frontier}. Over 2{,}000 random instances (exact
node recursion, $H=60$),
\cxInScope\ satisfy Assumption 2 in both stated forms. Within scope, the
ordered-marginals inequality fails in \cxUpper\ instances in its upper
half and \cxTwoSided\ in its full two-sided form; single crossing fails
at a comparable rate, with up to \cxCrossMax\ sign changes
in one instance. The violations occur deep in the long-horizon regime where the optimal
map already follows the primitive route, sit
nine orders above float noise, and an independent audit reproduced every
count and verified the saved examples with separate code. Three complete
counterexample instances with full primitives, each certified in scope,
are in \texttt{output/p25\_ordered\_marginals.json} in the repository
above, ready for direct reconstruction. The constructive reading: the exact ladder and the
interval conjecture need a primitive condition defining a subclass, and
that search is now example-guided rather than open-ended.


\section{Why the Table 1 intercepts look uniform}

The concern was that the category intercepts are almost the same constant
across items, and that after the sigmoid even their differences stop
mattering. Both halves now have answers. The flatness is largely
aggregation: refitting with subcategory intercepts (\subN\ subcategories
with sufficient data), the spread is \subSpread\ log-odds (\subPspread\
between the 5th and 95th percentiles), five times the category-level
0.85. The world is not flat; averaging heterogeneous article types into
14 categories flattened it, which argues for a finer action space in the
next dataset round. And the second half of the concern is exactly what
the phase map in Section 1 establishes: even a sixfold intercept spread
buys almost nothing at our $\kappa$, because with weak alignment
discrimination the sigmoid compresses whatever differences exist. The
two observations are one fact: consumption probabilities are
near-uniform because $\kappa$ is small, and the intercepts merely look
uniform because of aggregation.

A separate new fact surfaced by the same round, not raised in the
meeting but worth the group's attention: the transition is not one
$\rho$. Per-category profiling gives $\rho_c\approx0.05$ for news,
sports, and food, 0.02 for finance and entertainment, and
$\rho_c\approx0$ for travel and health (likelihood ratio \rhoTravelLR\
against the common value for travel alone). The pattern reads as
recency-driven versus need-driven content and strengthens the
persistent-interest interpretation of $\rho$.

\section{Resistance and the index, revised}

\textbf{Resistance: exists, and small, which is what the theory needs.}
On the full impression scale, jointly estimated with $\rho$, backfire is
decisively present (the simplex-native proportional-redistribution form
is data-preferred, likelihood ratio 1{,}489) with a per-impression
magnitude of 0.002 to 0.01, attention-diluted, far too small to threaten
the routing structure. Its policy value is material only when paired with
$\kappa\ge5$. Proposal: adopt it as a perturbation term in the
proportional form, keep the no-resistance case as the solved baseline,
and cite the measured magnitude as the smallness justification.

\textbf{Index policy: the route is the missing potential.} The route
value $W$ supplies a potential with a certified error bound where the
earlier geometric guess reached 97\% of oracle and the myopic-payoff
potential failed. The benchmark to beat remains estimate-then-plug-in
planning, near-oracle whenever a few persistent intercepts are the only
unknowns, so the index's case should be built where plug-in struggles:
state uncertainty or short-lived items, on the route-based $\Phi$.
